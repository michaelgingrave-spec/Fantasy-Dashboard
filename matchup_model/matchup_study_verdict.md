## Verdict (written 2026-10-15 from the tables below; re-running refreshes the tables, not this text)

**No bettable edge yet. The matchup and route-type tables add nothing for WR/TE, the WR/TE usage trend is already in the lines, and the best lead (the RB usage trend) is real but worth about the vig.**

1. **WR/TE matchup mismatches: no information, in any cut the Data Suite offers.** Alignment (wide / slot / inline) x what the defense allows from each alignment, man/zone, 1-high/2-high, personnel, depth of target and *route type* (his mix of slants, outs, digs, hitches, comebacks, corners, posts, gos, crossers, screens and flats x what the defense gives up on each) were all tried on four seasons (8,456 player-weeks). Adding them to a player's own profile never raises out-of-sample AUC for targets, receptions, yards, route share, yards per route or targets per route: 0 of 60 intervals clears zero and 20 are entirely below it (increments -0.013 to +0.011), including when only a short list fixed in advance is used. 660 best-vs-worst-matchup decile tests (246 of them route-type) land where chance puts them (7.7% at p < 0.05, best Benjamini-Hochberg q 0.39); a true gap of about 7 points would have shown.
2. **His own usage trend is real, and for WR/TE the books price it.** Out of sample the compact usage model scores AUC 0.62 on targets, 0.64 on route share and 0.58 on receiving yards (top-score decile beats his normal 67-72% of the time vs 26-30% in the bottom decile). Set against a full season of 2025 DraftKings and FanDuel closing lines (about 1,600 lines per market, bought from the Odds API) the book had already moved its line by 0.09 to 0.13 SD per SD of score, and what was left is +0.02 (rec yds) and +0.04 (receptions), intervals about +/-0.06; on the 2026 lines (about 630 each) +0.00 and +0.01. Nothing to bet.
3. **RBs.** Soft run defense (H2) and defense zone rate (H3) are dead: the closing lines already moved by the old effect. The **usage trend (H1) is the one that is not fully in the line**: on 612 DraftKings closing lines of 2025 the result beat the line by +0.14 SD per SD of score [+0.07, +0.21] (FanDuel the same; both halves of the season; almost all of it when usage is RISING). But it is a mean-yards effect on a skewed stat: it moves P(over) by about +3 points per SD, roughly the vig, and following the score at the posted price returned +0.5% per bet [-11, +12] at k = 1 (211 bets), +2% at k = 1.5. Tracked, not bet; the forward weeks and a bet-level rule decide (registry at the bottom).
4. **The 2025 closing lines lean toward the OVER**: only 45-47% of rec yds / receptions lines went over against 49-50% priced (-3 to -4 points, in both halves of the season and at both books), RB receptions -6. A blind UNDER earned between -3% (RB rush yards) and +4.5% (RB receptions) at the posted prices, every interval including 0: the vig absorbs most of the tilt, but it does mean OVER bets start 3-4 points behind and UNDER bets a little ahead.
5. **Our own projection does not beat the close** for WR/TE (z coefficient +0.03 to +0.08 per SD, intervals +/-0.3); for RB rush yards it is +0.4 per SD [-0.04, +0.96] (partly in-sample for 2025), the one place worth watching. Defense features only clearly predict RB efficiency (yards per carry: AUC 0.51 -> 0.54), which is not a market.

**Data note:** the first version of this study had to drop 2025 for WR/TE because that export had been cut to the top 1,500 rows by yards (it overstated 2025 YPRR by 30% and fed the Matchup Machine too). It was re-pulled with the week filter applied before the cap; the conclusions did not change.

**What this means for betting:** no change to the model or the app. A yes/no "will he beat his normal" call is possible (AUC 0.58-0.64 on usage), but it only pays where the line has not absorbed it, and on closing lines that is nowhere clear of the vig yet. I would not buy more historical lines for this: the best lead is about the size of the vig, so even twice the sample could not show a profit.
