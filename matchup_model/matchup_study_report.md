# Matchup mismatches and usage trends: can they tell us who will beat his normal?

Generated by `python -m matchup_model.opp.matchup_study`. Everything below is out-of-sample (leave-one-season-out) with player-clustered bootstrap intervals unless it says otherwise; nothing here changes the shipped projections.

## Verdict (written 2026-10-15 from the tables below; re-running refreshes the tables, not this text)

**The matchup and route-type tables do not find a WR/TE edge. The one real signal, a player's own recent usage, is already in the WR/TE lines. Two RB signals are left to watch; neither is bettable yet.**

1. **WR/TE matchup mismatches: no information, in any cut the Data Suite offers.** Alignment (wide / slot / inline) x what the defense allows from each alignment, man/zone, 1-high/2-high, personnel, depth of target and, last, *route type* (his mix of slants, outs, digs, hitches, comebacks, corners, posts, gos, crossers, screens and flats x what the defense gives up on each) were all tried on four seasons (8,456 player-weeks). Adding them to a player's own profile never raises out-of-sample AUC for targets, receptions, yards, route share, yards per route or targets per route: 0 of 60 intervals clears zero and 20 are entirely below it (increments -0.013 to +0.011), including when only a short list fixed in advance is used. 660 best-vs-worst-matchup decile tests (246 of them route-type) land where chance puts them (7.7% at p < 0.05, best Benjamini-Hochberg q 0.39); a true gap of about 7 points would have shown.
2. **His own usage trend is real, and for WR/TE the books already price it.** Out of sample the compact usage model scores AUC 0.62 on targets, 0.64 on route share and 0.58 on receiving yards (top-score decile beats his normal 67-72% of the time on targets and route share vs 26-30% in the bottom decile) and cuts raw-stat error 1.5-3.6% in every season. On the 2026 lines (weeks 1-5, about 630 each for rec yds and receptions) the signal replicates (+0.12 and +0.18 SD of the stat per SD of score vs our projection; history +0.16 and +0.20) and the book moved its line by +0.12 and +0.18, leaving +0.00 and +0.01 (interval about +/-0.1): nothing left to bet.
3. **RBs:** (a) *own usage trend*: strong and stable (+0.19 to +0.31 SD per SD in every season 2022-25, AUC 0.61-0.63) and the book does not move its line with it, but 2026 weeks 1-5 (260 lines) show only +0.08 vs our projection (interval -0.06 to +0.22), so it is neither confirmed nor refuted: the one thing worth a forward test. (b) *soft run defense -> more rush yards*: +2.5 to +3.8 yards per SD in 2022-24 (also from nflverse alone), +0.5 in 2025, about zero in 2026 weeks 1-5, and the book already moves its line ~60% of the old effect: it looks gone, do not bet it. (c) *defense zone rate -> RB receptions*: real but tiny (+0.08 receptions per SD).
4. **The one place defense features clearly predict is RB efficiency** (yards per carry above his trailing average: AUC 0.51 -> 0.54, best vs worst run-defense decile 60% vs 43%, BH q 0.04), but it fades in 2025-26 and yards per carry is not a market.

**Data note:** the first version of this study had to drop 2025 for WR/TE because that export had been cut to the top 1,500 rows by yards (it overstated 2025 YPRR by 30% and fed the Matchup Machine too). It was re-pulled with the week filter applied before the cap and this version uses it; the conclusions did not change.

**What this means for betting:** no change to the model or the app. A binary "will he beat his normal" call is possible (AUC 0.58-0.64 on usage), but it is only worth money where the line has not already absorbed it, and so far that is untested territory for RBs only. Forward-test H1 on weeks 6+ (registry at the bottom). The data that would settle H1 faster is historical sportsbook prop lines, which FantasyPoints does not publish.

## How to read it

'Beats his normal' means: actual > the conditional MEDIAN of that stat given his projected level (the shipped model's own walk-forward projection), the median learned on the other seasons only. That makes every label ~50/50 at every level, so projection level, skew and ceilings cannot help and any AUC above 0.5 is information the level does not contain. Feature sets are nested: **B0** his projected level only; **B1** + his own usage / efficiency profile and trends; **B2** + the opposing defense; **B3** + the mismatch (his profile x their weakness); **B4** + ONLY a short pre-specified list of mismatch features (so noise cannot drown them); **B5** + ONLY the short route-type mismatch list (his route mix x what the defense allows on each route type); **B6** + every route-type feature (route mix, allowed by route type and depth bucket, mismatch). B0 is exactly what the label was centred on, so its AUC should sit at 0.5: that is the check on the labels. A feature set earns its place only if the AUC increment's interval clears 0. B2 and B3 add ~100 defense features, so they can lose to noise even if a few carry a little signal; B4 is the fair version of that test: only the short list fixed in advance (WR/TE: mm_align_yprr, mm_align_tprr, mm_man_yprr, mm_zone_yprr, mm_sh_yprr, mm_th_yprr, mm_depth_adot, d_all_yprr_rel, d_all_tprr_rel, d_all_adot_dev; RB: mm_concept_ypc, mm_run_plus, mm_box_hit, d_ypc_rel, d_succ_dev, d_stuff_dev, d_zone_dev). B5 and B6 are the same two tests for the route-type features (WR/TE only; short list: rm_yprr, rm_tprr, rm_deep_yprr, rm_int_yprr, rm_short_yprr).

## Caveats

- **The 2025 WR/TE tables were re-pulled on 2026-10-15.** The first export had been cut by the Data Suite's 1,500-row cap to the top rows by receiving yards (no quiet games; it overstated 2025 YPRR by ~30%). `weekly_pull.backfill_tables` now filters weeks on the server (`regWeeks=`) and `filtered_seasons` would catch a repeat. Every trailing window is capped at the previous season, so a score needs >= 3 prior games inside that window.
- **'Normal' is our own model.** The labels are centred on the shipped projection's level, whose constants were fit on 2023-25: that can tilt levels but cannot create a label imbalance (the median is re-fit on the other seasons), and B0 sitting at 0.5 checks it.
- **The 2026 market tests are small** (a few hundred lines, weeks 1-5) and 'beyond the price' uses the book's stored no-vig probability, not each book's own number.
- **Many tests.** The WR/TE and RB tails alone are several hundred; Benjamini-Hochberg is applied within each group, and the pre-specified RB hypotheses are the only ones that were fixed before the outcomes were looked at.
- **Not covered:** QBs, anytime-TD and passing markets, injuries/teammate-out usage shifts, game script, and the defender-level (cornerback / safety) Data Suite tables, which would be needed for receiver-vs-defender matchups.
