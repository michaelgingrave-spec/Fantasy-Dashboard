# Matchup mismatches and usage trends: can they tell us who will beat his normal?

Generated by `python -m matchup_model.opp.matchup_study`. Everything below is out-of-sample (leave-one-season-out) with player-clustered bootstrap intervals unless it says otherwise; nothing here changes the shipped projections.

## Verdict (written 2026-10-15 from the tables below; re-running refreshes the tables, not this text)

**No bettable edge yet. The matchup and route-type tables add nothing for WR/TE, the WR/TE usage trend is already in the lines, and the RB usage trend is real but worth about the vig. The one mechanical lead is price shopping between DraftKings and FanDuel (point 6), and it is unreplicated.**

1. **WR/TE matchup mismatches: no information, in any cut the Data Suite offers.** Alignment (wide / slot / inline) x what the defense allows from each alignment, man/zone, 1-high/2-high, personnel, depth of target and *route type* (his mix of slants, outs, digs, hitches, comebacks, corners, posts, gos, crossers, screens and flats x what the defense gives up on each) were all tried on four seasons (8,456 player-weeks). Adding them to a player's own profile never raises out-of-sample AUC for targets, receptions, yards, route share, yards per route or targets per route: 0 of 60 intervals clears zero and 20 are entirely below it (increments -0.013 to +0.011), including when only a short list fixed in advance is used. 660 best-vs-worst-matchup decile tests (246 of them route-type) land where chance puts them (7.7% at p < 0.05, best Benjamini-Hochberg q 0.39); a true gap of about 7 points would have shown.
2. **His own usage trend is real, and for WR/TE the books price it.** Out of sample the compact usage model scores AUC 0.62 on targets, 0.64 on route share and 0.58 on receiving yards (top-score decile beats his normal 67-72% of the time vs 26-30% in the bottom decile). Set against a full season of 2025 DraftKings and FanDuel closing lines (about 1,600 lines per market, bought from the Odds API) the book had already moved its line by 0.09 to 0.13 SD per SD of score, and what was left is +0.02 (rec yds) and +0.04 (receptions), intervals about +/-0.06; on the 2026 lines (about 630 each) +0.00 and +0.01. Nothing to bet. It is not a timing effect either: on the 2026 Wednesday opening lines (367 rec yds and 362 receptions lines, weeks 1-5) the opener already held the whole effect (+0.07 and +0.11 SD per SD of score, against +0.07 and +0.11 at the late line) and the market added nothing during the week (+0.008 [-0.010, +0.026] and +0.000 [-0.024, +0.024]), so there is no early-bet window.
3. **RBs.** Soft run defense (H2) and defense zone rate (H3) are dead: the closing lines already moved by the old effect. The **usage trend (H1) is the one that is not fully in the line**: on 612 DraftKings closing lines of 2025 the result beat the line by +0.14 SD per SD of score [+0.07, +0.21] (FanDuel the same; both halves of the season; almost all of it when usage is RISING). But it is a mean-yards effect on a skewed stat: it moves P(over) by about +3 points per SD, roughly the vig, and following the score at the posted price returned +0.5% per bet [-11, +12] at k = 1 (211 bets), +2% at k = 1.5. Tracked, not bet; the forward weeks and a bet-level rule decide (registry at the bottom).
4. **The 2025 closing lines lean toward the OVER**: only 45-47% of rec yds / receptions lines went over against 49-50% priced (-3 to -4 points, in both halves of the season and at both books), RB receptions -6. A blind UNDER earned between -3% (RB rush yards) and +4.5% (RB receptions) at the posted prices, every interval including 0: the vig absorbs most of the tilt, but it does mean OVER bets start 3-4 points behind and UNDER bets a little ahead.
5. **Our own projection does not beat the close** for WR/TE (z coefficient +0.03 to +0.08 per SD, intervals +/-0.3); for RB rush yards it is +0.4 per SD [-0.04, +0.96] (partly in-sample for 2025), the one place worth watching. Defense features only clearly predict RB efficiency (yards per carry: AUC 0.51 -> 0.54), which is not a market.
6. **DraftKings and FanDuel post different numbers on most yardage props. Taking the better number is the one mechanical gain; nothing else about the disagreement pays.** Of 4,992 closing props listed at both books the numbers differ on 65% of receiving-yard and 66% of rushing-yard props (by 2 or more on 20% and 24%) but on only 8% of receptions. Same-number arbitrage is essentially absent (2 of 2,933 props with the same number had best prices summing to under 100%), and 'middles' (OVER at the lower number plus UNDER at the higher) lose 2% to 4% per dollar staked: the result lands between the two numbers only 1.5% to 2.3% of the time on yardage props, and about 5% is needed to break even. What does pay is the single bet at the better number: the UNDER at the higher of the two numbers returned +5.4% per bet on receiving yards [+0.8, +10.1] (1,418 bets) and +1.6% on rushing yards [-6.6, +10.3] (466), against +1.3% and -0.8% for the UNDER at the lower number, so roughly +2 to +4 points for the same side at the better number. Every OVER leg on yardage lost 10% to 16% (the vig alone is about 4.5%). This is the closing-line lean toward the UNDER of point 4 plus the value of the better number, found in this one sample after about a dozen looks, with an interval that only just clears zero on receiving yards: a lead to replicate on another season and track forward, not an edge.
7. **FantasyPoints' own weekly projection is better than our number, and the books already hold it.** It is the one proprietary input that is not a function of games already played, so it is where an information edge would most plausibly live. On the 2,016 lines of 2026 weeks 2-5 that carry its number, per SD of the gap between its projection and ours (after our own nudge toward it), the result lands +0.156 SD of the stat [+0.103, +0.216] closer to FantasyPoints than to us, and the book's line had already moved +0.189 [+0.160, +0.226] toward it: what is left is -0.033 [-0.081, +0.025]. As a yes/no beyond the price and our own edge it is +2.1 points of P(over) per SD [-1.0, +5.5], under the 2.4 the vig takes. Same shape for rec yds, receptions and rush yds on their own. Registered as H5 with a fixed decision rule for weeks 12 and 18 (registry at the bottom); the early weeks ran on the original projections, so the forward weeks are the clean test.
8. **Where and on which side a bet is placed is worth more than any signal we found.** On the 2025 closing lines of both books, with our model's leans held fixed, the app's old placement (the book with the best price, at that book's own number) earned -5.8% per bet over 2,596 yardage bets [-9.3, -2.5], 67% of them OVER leans. Placing the same leans at the best number instead earned -4.6% [-8.0, -1.2], which is +1.25 points per bet [+0.62, +1.94] and is arithmetic, not forecasting. Also dropping the OVER leans earned +2.7% over the 859 UNDER yardage leans [-3.1, +8.1] and +6.5% over 1,009 UNDER receptions leans [+1.6, +11.8]. Ignoring our projection altogether, always betting the OVER at the best number lost -9.2% [-12.7, -5.9] and always betting the UNDER earned +1.3% [-2.2, +4.6]: the books shade the OVER side, and a model that mostly leans OVER pays for it on every bet. The app's own 2026 graded leans, an independent season, split the same way (UNDER leans won 53.2% and returned +1.6% at -110 over 733 lines, OVER leans won 48.7% and returned -7.0% over 1,710). So the UNDER side is about break-even rather than proven profitable (most intervals include 0), the idea was found in the 2025 sample, and pass props are not covered (the 2026 pass-yards OVERs ran the other way on 87 lines). The Prop Edges screen now defaults to UNDER leans only in rec yds, receptions and rush yds, placed at the best number; weeks 6-8 of its own graded record are the check.

**Data note:** the first version of this study had to drop 2025 for WR/TE because that export had been cut to the top 1,500 rows by yards (it overstated 2025 YPRR by 30% and fed the Matchup Machine too). It was re-pulled with the week filter applied before the cap; the conclusions did not change.

**How to read "no edge":** two different nulls. The matchup tables do not predict at all (the data adds nothing to a player's own numbers, so there is nothing for any book to have priced). The usage trend does predict and the books already hold it, even at Wednesday's opener. And the bet-level tests are blunt: a few hundred bets per signal can only rule out an edge worth more than roughly +8 to 13% ROI (see the power caveat at the bottom), so smaller edges are invisible in either direction; the line-movement tests are far tighter and are where to look first.

**What this means for betting:** no change to the model's projections. The one change the evidence supports is in how bets are placed and which side is shown (point 8). A yes/no "will he beat his normal" call is possible (AUC 0.58-0.64 on usage), but it only pays where the line has not absorbed it, and on closing lines that is nowhere clear of the vig yet. I would not buy more historical lines to chase the usage trend: that lead is about the size of the vig, so even twice the sample could not show a profit. The cross-book lead is different, it is a price effect, and another season of closing lines (2024 receiving yards, about 2,100 Odds API credits) is what would confirm or kill it. Separately, where DraftKings and FanDuel differ on a yardage prop, betting at the better number costs nothing and is worth a couple of points per bet whatever the side.

## How to read it

'Beats his normal' means: actual > the conditional MEDIAN of that stat given his projected level (the shipped model's own walk-forward projection), the median learned on the other seasons only. That makes every label ~50/50 at every level, so projection level, skew and ceilings cannot help and any AUC above 0.5 is information the level does not contain. Feature sets are nested: **B0** his projected level only; **B1** + his own usage / efficiency profile and trends; **B2** + the opposing defense; **B3** + the mismatch (his profile x their weakness); **B4** + ONLY a short pre-specified list of mismatch features (so noise cannot drown them); **B5** + ONLY the short route-type mismatch list (his route mix x what the defense allows on each route type); **B6** + every route-type feature (route mix, allowed by route type and depth bucket, mismatch). B0 is exactly what the label was centred on, so its AUC should sit at 0.5: that is the check on the labels. A feature set earns its place only if the AUC increment's interval clears 0. B2 and B3 add ~100 defense features, so they can lose to noise even if a few carry a little signal; B4 is the fair version of that test: only the short list fixed in advance (WR/TE: mm_align_yprr, mm_align_tprr, mm_man_yprr, mm_zone_yprr, mm_sh_yprr, mm_th_yprr, mm_depth_adot, d_all_yprr_rel, d_all_tprr_rel, d_all_adot_dev; RB: mm_concept_ypc, mm_run_plus, mm_box_hit, d_ypc_rel, d_succ_dev, d_stuff_dev, d_zone_dev). B5 and B6 are the same two tests for the route-type features (WR/TE only; short list: rm_yprr, rm_tprr, rm_deep_yprr, rm_int_yprr, rm_short_yprr).

## Is the signal already in the book's line?

All effects are per +1 SD of the signal, in SDs of the stat (rush yards, receptions, ...). **History** is what 2022-25 says the signal is worth: the result minus OUR projection, out of fold. On real lines that splits in two: (result minus our projection) = (how far the book moved its line from our projection with the signal) + (result minus the line). The last term is what is LEFT for a bettor: 0 if the line already contains the signal, equal to the history if it contains none of it. ### Does FantasyPoints' own projection know more than our number? (2026 lines as logged)

FantasyPoints' weekly export is the one proprietary input that is not a function of games already played. The app already nudges our number toward it (up to half the gap once they differ by more than 12%), and stores the remaining gap as `role_ratio` = FantasyPoints / our nudged number. ## Caveats

- **The 2025 WR/TE tables were re-pulled on 2026-10-15.** The first export had been cut by the Data Suite's 1,500-row cap to the top rows by receiving yards (no quiet games; it overstated 2025 YPRR by ~30%). `weekly_pull.backfill_tables` now filters weeks on the server (`regWeeks=`) and `filtered_seasons` would catch a repeat. Every trailing window is capped at the previous season, so a score needs >= 3 prior games inside that window.
- **'Our projection beats the close' is not clean for 2025.** The RB and WR/TE constants of the shipped model were fit on 2023-25 box scores, so the 2025 rows of that table are partly in-sample (the signal tests are not affected: they compare the result with the LINE, and the scores are out of fold). Treat 2025 there as an upper bound; 2026 is the clean test.
- **'Normal' is our own model.** The labels are centred on the shipped projection's level, whose constants were fit on 2023-25: that can tilt levels but cannot create a label imbalance (the median is re-fit on the other seasons), and B0 sitting at 0.5 checks it.
- **Which lines.** 2025 is DraftKings and FanDuel CLOSING lines (a snapshot 5 minutes before kickoff, weeks 5-18; 205 of 208 games had props) with each book's own two prices; 2026 is the lines as logged by the app (a few hundred per market, weeks 1-5) with the stored no-vig probability. A bettor who bets earlier than the close faces a softer line, so the close is the hardest test.
- **What the bet-level tests can and cannot rule out.** At 200-500 bets per signal an ROI interval is about +/-8 to 13 points wide (the payoff of one bet has a standard deviation of about 0.95 units), so a true +5% edge would show up as 'entirely above zero' about 1 time in 5 at 500 bets (a true +3%: 1 in 10), and confirming +3% with 80% power takes about 8,000 bets. 'No improvement' therefore means no edge worth more than roughly +8 to 13% ROI; smaller ones are invisible in either direction. The line-movement tests are far tighter (+/-0.02 SD) because a line move is not a coin flip, which is why they are worth running first.
- **FantasyPoints history.** FantasyPoints did not archive past weekly projections, so H5 exists only for 2026 weeks 2-5 as the app logged them (about 2,000 lines of about 900 player-weeks; one player's lines in a week share one gap, so the intervals are clustered by player). Those weeks ran on the original (v1) projections, which over-projected WR/TE, so part of FantasyPoints' edge over our number there was bias our later recalibration removes: the forward weeks (v3 projections) are the clean test. The raw log ratio, unclipped and uncentred, gives the same picture (+0.45 per unit beyond the price and our edge [-0.04, +0.96]).
- **Many tests.** The WR/TE and RB tails alone are several hundred; Benjamini-Hochberg is applied within each group, and the pre-specified RB hypotheses are the only ones that were fixed before the outcomes were looked at.
- **Not covered:** QBs, anytime-TD and passing markets, injuries/teammate-out usage shifts, game script, and the defender-level (cornerback / safety) Data Suite tables, which would be needed for receiver-vs-defender matchups.
