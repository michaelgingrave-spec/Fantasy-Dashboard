# Matchup mismatches and usage trends: can they tell us who will beat his normal?

Generated by `python -m matchup_model.opp.matchup_study`. Everything below is out-of-sample (leave-one-season-out) with player-clustered bootstrap intervals unless it says otherwise; nothing here changes the shipped projections.

## Verdict (written 2026-10-15 from the tables below; re-running refreshes the tables, not this text)

**No bettable edge yet. The matchup and route-type tables add nothing for WR/TE, the WR/TE usage trend is already in the lines, and the best lead (the RB usage trend) is real but worth about the vig.**

1. **WR/TE matchup mismatches: no information, in any cut the Data Suite offers.** Alignment (wide / slot / inline) x what the defense allows from each alignment, man/zone, 1-high/2-high, personnel, depth of target and *route type* (his mix of slants, outs, digs, hitches, comebacks, corners, posts, gos, crossers, screens and flats x what the defense gives up on each) were all tried on four seasons (8,456 player-weeks). Adding them to a player's own profile never raises out-of-sample AUC for targets, receptions, yards, route share, yards per route or targets per route: 0 of 60 intervals clears zero and 20 are entirely below it (increments -0.013 to +0.011), including when only a short list fixed in advance is used. 660 best-vs-worst-matchup decile tests (246 of them route-type) land where chance puts them (7.7% at p < 0.05, best Benjamini-Hochberg q 0.39); a true gap of about 7 points would have shown.
2. **His own usage trend is real, and for WR/TE the books price it.** Out of sample the compact usage model scores AUC 0.62 on targets, 0.64 on route share and 0.58 on receiving yards (top-score decile beats his normal 67-72% of the time vs 26-30% in the bottom decile). Set against a full season of 2025 DraftKings and FanDuel closing lines (about 1,600 lines per market, bought from the Odds API) the book had already moved its line by 0.09 to 0.13 SD per SD of score, and what was left is +0.02 (rec yds) and +0.04 (receptions), intervals about +/-0.06; on the 2026 lines (about 630 each) +0.00 and +0.01. Nothing to bet.
3. **RBs.** Soft run defense (H2) and defense zone rate (H3) are dead: the closing lines already moved by the old effect. The **usage trend (H1) is the one that is not fully in the line**: on 612 DraftKings closing lines of 2025 the result beat the line by +0.14 SD per SD of score [+0.07, +0.21] (FanDuel the same; both halves of the season; almost all of it when usage is RISING). But it is a mean-yards effect on a skewed stat: it moves P(over) by about +3 points per SD, roughly the vig, and following the score at the posted price returned +0.5% per bet [-11, +12] at k = 1 (211 bets), +2% at k = 1.5. Tracked, not bet; the forward weeks and a bet-level rule decide (registry at the bottom).
4. **The 2025 closing lines lean toward the OVER**: only 45-47% of rec yds / receptions lines went over against 49-50% priced (-3 to -4 points, in both halves of the season and at both books), RB receptions -6. A blind UNDER earned between -3% (RB rush yards) and +4.5% (RB receptions) at the posted prices, every interval including 0: the vig absorbs most of the tilt, but it does mean OVER bets start 3-4 points behind and UNDER bets a little ahead.
5. **Our own projection does not beat the close** for WR/TE (z coefficient +0.03 to +0.08 per SD, intervals +/-0.3); for RB rush yards it is +0.4 per SD [-0.04, +0.96] (partly in-sample for 2025), the one place worth watching. Defense features only clearly predict RB efficiency (yards per carry: AUC 0.51 -> 0.54), which is not a market.

**Data note:** the first version of this study had to drop 2025 for WR/TE because that export had been cut to the top 1,500 rows by yards (it overstated 2025 YPRR by 30% and fed the Matchup Machine too). It was re-pulled with the week filter applied before the cap; the conclusions did not change.

**What this means for betting:** no change to the model or the app. A yes/no "will he beat his normal" call is possible (AUC 0.58-0.64 on usage), but it only pays where the line has not absorbed it, and on closing lines that is nowhere clear of the vig yet. I would not buy more historical lines for this: the best lead is about the size of the vig, so even twice the sample could not show a profit.

## How to read it

'Beats his normal' means: actual > the conditional MEDIAN of that stat given his projected level (the shipped model's own walk-forward projection), the median learned on the other seasons only. That makes every label ~50/50 at every level, so projection level, skew and ceilings cannot help and any AUC above 0.5 is information the level does not contain. Feature sets are nested: **B0** his projected level only; **B1** + his own usage / efficiency profile and trends; **B2** + the opposing defense; **B3** + the mismatch (his profile x their weakness); **B4** + ONLY a short pre-specified list of mismatch features (so noise cannot drown them); **B5** + ONLY the short route-type mismatch list (his route mix x what the defense allows on each route type); **B6** + every route-type feature (route mix, allowed by route type and depth bucket, mismatch). B0 is exactly what the label was centred on, so its AUC should sit at 0.5: that is the check on the labels. A feature set earns its place only if the AUC increment's interval clears 0. B2 and B3 add ~100 defense features, so they can lose to noise even if a few carry a little signal; B4 is the fair version of that test: only the short list fixed in advance (WR/TE: mm_align_yprr, mm_align_tprr, mm_man_yprr, mm_zone_yprr, mm_sh_yprr, mm_th_yprr, mm_depth_adot, d_all_yprr_rel, d_all_tprr_rel, d_all_adot_dev; RB: mm_concept_ypc, mm_run_plus, mm_box_hit, d_ypc_rel, d_succ_dev, d_stuff_dev, d_zone_dev). B5 and B6 are the same two tests for the route-type features (WR/TE only; short list: rm_yprr, rm_tprr, rm_deep_yprr, rm_int_yprr, rm_short_yprr).

## Forward-test registry (frozen 2026-10-15, rule tightened the same day before any forward week was seen)

One test per hypothesis, no re-fitting, on 2026 lines from week 6 on. After each week is graded run `python -m matchup_model.opp.matchup_study --forward` (a few minutes) and the 'forward' rows above fill in. 'Result minus the LINE' is 0 when the book already contains the signal, but it is measured in MEAN yards, and rushing yards are right-skewed: a mean shift of +0.4 SD can be only +3 points of P(over) (the 2025 RB result: the top score quintile beat the line by +0.45 SD on average but went over only 52.9% of the time). So the rule is in bet terms: a signal is acted on only if, over at least 300 forward bets at the posted price following it with k = 1, the 95% interval of ROI per bet is entirely above 0 AND the 'beyond-the-price' coefficient moves P(over) by more than the vig (2.4 points per SD). ## Caveats

- **The 2025 WR/TE tables were re-pulled on 2026-10-15.** The first export had been cut by the Data Suite's 1,500-row cap to the top rows by receiving yards (no quiet games; it overstated 2025 YPRR by ~30%). `weekly_pull.backfill_tables` now filters weeks on the server (`regWeeks=`) and `filtered_seasons` would catch a repeat. Every trailing window is capped at the previous season, so a score needs >= 3 prior games inside that window.
- **'Our projection beats the close' is not clean for 2025.** The RB and WR/TE constants of the shipped model were fit on 2023-25 box scores, so the 2025 rows of that table are partly in-sample (the signal tests are not affected: they compare the result with the LINE, and the scores are out of fold). Treat 2025 there as an upper bound; 2026 is the clean test.
- **'Normal' is our own model.** The labels are centred on the shipped projection's level, whose constants were fit on 2023-25: that can tilt levels but cannot create a label imbalance (the median is re-fit on the other seasons), and B0 sitting at 0.5 checks it.
- **Which lines.** 2025 is DraftKings and FanDuel CLOSING lines (a snapshot 5 minutes before kickoff, weeks 5-18; 205 of 208 games had props) with each book's own two prices; 2026 is the lines as logged by the app (a few hundred per market, weeks 1-5) with the stored no-vig probability. A bettor who bets earlier than the close faces a softer line, so the close is the hardest test.
- **Many tests.** The WR/TE and RB tails alone are several hundred; Benjamini-Hochberg is applied within each group, and the pre-specified RB hypotheses are the only ones that were fixed before the outcomes were looked at.
- **Not covered:** QBs, anytime-TD and passing markets, injuries/teammate-out usage shifts, game script, and the defender-level (cornerback / safety) Data Suite tables, which would be needed for receiver-vs-defender matchups.
